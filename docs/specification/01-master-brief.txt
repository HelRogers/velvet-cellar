THE VELVET CELLAR

Master Website Design, Development & Functionality Brief

1. Project overview

I want to create a premium, immersive, private event website for a joint 21st birthday celebration and interactive 1920s murder mystery party for approximately 24 guests.

The website will be called The Velvet Cellar, the fictional underground speakeasy in which our murder mystery takes place.

This should not feel like a conventional birthday invitation, wedding website, event management platform or generic party website. It should feel as though guests have been personally invited into an exclusive, clandestine establishment in 1925, where glamour, champagne, jazz, gambling, secrets and suspicion coexist.

The website will act as the central hub for the entire party experience, from receiving the original invitation through to discovering character identities, preparing costumes, accessing confidential murder mystery booklets, receiving announcements, voting on the night and eventually sharing photographs.

Guests will receive a single website link before the party. This same website and URL must remain in use throughout the entire process, with new information and functionality becoming available at different stages.

The experience should be exceptionally well designed, atmospheric, functional, responsive and easy to navigate, while remaining as inexpensive as possible to operate.

3. Murder mystery context

The party uses a purchased murder mystery game called Death in the Roaring 20s by The Obituary Club, which is being customised for our particular group of friends.

Each guest will be assigned an individual fictional character, with their own identity, personality, background, relationships, secrets, objectives and instructions for the evening.

The murder mystery takes place across a series of guided rounds, during which guests will interact with each other, investigate evidence, discover clues, solve puzzles or coded messages where applicable, question suspects, exchange information and attempt to determine who committed the murder.

The evening concludes with accusations, voting and the final reveal.

Awards include:

* Best Detective
* Best Dressed
* Best Actor

An important feature of this particular murder mystery is that even the person playing the murderer does not know they are the murderer until the final reveal.

ABSOLUTE SPOILER PROTECTION REQUIREMENT

I am participating in the murder mystery myself and do not want to know the solution.

Do not reveal, infer, summarise, hint at or identify the murderer, their motive or the final solution at any stage of website development.

Never upload the final narration, final reveal or solution documents to the guest website, public storage, website source code, database or downloadable assets.

All initial character introductions must be based exclusively on approved, spoiler-free public character information. Do not use private character backgrounds, objectives, relationships, evidence or confidential information when creating initial introductions.

I will supply the individual approved character files and summaries when required.

PART TWO: CREATIVE DIRECTION

4. Overall design vision

The website should feel like a combination of:

* An exclusive 1920s private members’ club.
* Luxury vintage invitation stationery.
* A prohibition-era underground speakeasy.
* A sophisticated murder mystery.
* A confidential archive of personal dossiers.
* An editorial-style vintage fashion and lifestyle magazine.

The primary aesthetic should be luxurious 1920s speakeasy glamour. Murder mystery references should add intrigue and tension without overwhelming the elegance.

The website must feel bespoke and carefully art-directed, rather than like a standard template with vintage colours applied.

Avoid:

* Generic Great Gatsby templates.
* Excessive glitter and gold effects.
* Cartoon gangsters or novelty murder mystery graphics.
* Halloween-style horror.
* Excessively bright colours.
* Modern corporate website styling.
* Generic rounded SaaS-style cards throughout the guest-facing experience.
* Stock icons that clash with the period aesthetic.
* Overly busy layouts.
* Excessive animations that make the site difficult to navigate.
* Obvious AI-generated-looking imagery.

The desired feeling is exclusive, atmospheric, sophisticated, mysterious, cinematic and slightly illicit.

Occasionally use typewriter-inspired or handwritten lettering for:

* Confidential stamps.
* Dossier reference numbers.

Do not use decorative fonts for lengthy body text if this compromises readability.

Use appropriately licensed fonts, preferably free fonts that can be hosted reliably.

10. Section one: The Sealed Invitation

This is the most important first impression and must receive particular attention.

When a guest first opens the website, they should be presented with an immersive, almost full-screen scene.

The scene should contain:

* A dark burgundy velvet or similarly luxurious background.
* An elegant gold or antique champagne-coloured envelope.
* Realistic paper texture and soft shadows.
* A deep burgundy wax seal.
* An embossed V or VC monogram on the seal.
* Subtle atmospheric lighting.
* Restrained ornamental details.

Suggested introductory copy:

PRIVATE CORRESPONDENCE

You are cordially invited.

Break the seal to enter.

The envelope or wax seal should clearly indicate that it is interactive.

When tapped or clicked, create a smooth, realistic animation in which:

1. The wax seal appears to break or release.
2. The envelope flap lifts naturally.
3. The invitation slides slowly out of the envelope.
4. The front of the supplied invitation is revealed.
5. The invitation settles into a clear, readable position.
6. The guest can manually turn the card over to reveal the back.

The animation should feel like interacting with a physical luxury invitation.

Use layered HTML, CSS, SVG, JavaScript or another appropriate deterministic animation technique where possible, rather than relying on generative video that may distort the invitation artwork.

The envelope opening should feel elegant and deliberate, not excessively fast.

Invitation viewer requirements:

* Display the original front and back images in high quality.
* Allow guests to flip between the front and back as many times as they wish.
* Use a smooth, realistic card-flip transition.
* Include a clear indication that the card can be turned over.
* Allow tapping or clicking to zoom or enlarge the invitation.
* Provide an accessible fullscreen or expanded viewing option.
* Never crop important information.
* Do not automatically advance between the front and back.
* Do not impose a reading time limit.
* Include a way to replay the envelope opening.
* Allow users to skip the animation if desired.
* Respect reduced-motion accessibility preferences.

Once the invitation has been opened, reveal or provide access to the main website beneath it.

On subsequent visits, remember locally that the invitation has already been opened where possible. Guests should be able to go directly to the main website without repeatedly sitting through the animation.

The opening animation must remain available to replay.

17. Individual character security and access codes

I would ideally like every guest to have their own unique access code to protect their confidential character materials.

The priority is to prevent guests from accidentally or deliberately reading another person’s private booklet and spoiling the mystery.

However, this is a small private event, and I trust my friends. Security should be implemented sensibly without unnecessarily increasing costs or delaying the website’s development.

Use the following order of preference.

Preferred approach: Secure individual access codes

When a guest attempts to open their full confidential booklet, display a themed access screen.

Suggested wording:

RESTRICTED ACCESS

This dossier contains strictly confidential information. Enter your personal access code to retrieve its contents.

Each guest should receive a unique, randomly generated code, which I will send to them privately.

If the correct code is entered:

* Validate the code securely.
* Grant access only to that specific guest’s authorised document.
* Display or provide an authorised download of their booklet.
* Show an atmospheric confirmation, such as Identity verified. Dossier unlocked.

If an incorrect code is entered:

* Deny access.
* Display a clear but appropriately themed error.
* Limit repeated guessing attempts where supported.

Suggested technical requirements:

* Server-side validation.
* Private file storage.
* Secure access controls.
* Appropriately hashed access codes or equivalent secure authentication.
* Rate limiting.
* Short-lived authorised file URLs where appropriate.
* No confidential documents or access-code lists exposed in publicly downloadable website files or client-side code.
* The ability to regenerate or revoke an individual guest’s access code.

A free-tier service such as Supabase may be considered, or another suitable secure alternative.

Do not introduce a complex membership system with unnecessary account creation, email verification or multiple login steps for every guest.

Preferred fallback: Individually encrypted PDFs

If a fully integrated access-code system is unnecessarily complex, unreliable or expensive, use individually password-encrypted PDF booklets.

Each guest’s introductory character page can remain accessible to invited guests, while their actual confidential booklet requires a unique password.

Use proper PDF encryption, not simply a visual password overlay or editing restriction.

This is an acceptable alternative if it makes the website substantially easier to build and maintain.

Final fallback: Trust-based access

If neither secure method can be implemented reliably within the available budget, a trust-based system may be considered, subject to my approval.

Before opening a booklet, display a warning such as:

A WORD OF WARNING…

The contents of this dossier are strictly confidential. Opening another guest’s file may spoil the mystery, not only for yourself but for everyone attending.

Honour the secrets of The Velvet Cellar. Please access only your own assigned dossier.

Require the guest to acknowledge the warning before proceeding.

Be transparent that this approach does not provide technical security.

Do not implement a fake password screen or claim that a publicly accessible document is securely protected.

If secure dossier functionality is not ready for the initial website release, launch with the public character introductions and sealed booklet placeholders. Do not delay the entire invitation launch unnecessarily.

PART FOUR: TECHNICAL REQUIREMENTS

25. Responsive design

The website must be fully responsive and carefully designed for:

* iPhone and Android mobile phones.
* Small and large mobile screens.
* iPads and other tablets.
* Tablet portrait and landscape orientations.
* Laptop computers.
* Large desktop monitors.

Mobile-first does not mean mobile-only.

The desktop and tablet layouts must feel intentionally designed for their respective screen sizes.

On mobile:

* Use a vertical, immersive scrolling experience.
* Keep the invitation large and readable.
* Ensure buttons and controls are easy to tap.
* Avoid horizontal scrolling.
* Provide convenient navigation.
* Ensure PDF and image viewing works reliably.

On tablets:

* Use larger invitation artwork.
* Take advantage of additional horizontal space.
* Use multi-column character registers.
* Create more spacious editorial mood board layouts.
* Support both portrait and landscape viewing.

On desktop:

* Use generous spacing and sophisticated compositions.
* Make appropriate use of wider imagery.
* Consider carefully designed two-column sections.
* Avoid simply displaying a narrow mobile layout in the middle of the screen.
* Keep long text readable through sensible maximum text widths.

Test the website at representative widths, including approximately 360px, 390px, 768px, 1024px, 1440px and larger desktop sizes.

No text, artwork, buttons or section headings should overlap, overflow or become partially obscured.

26. Navigation and usability

The website should be immersive but never confusing.

Requirements:

* Clear navigation menu.
* Section anchor links.
* Functional back-to-register navigation from character pages.
* A discreet return-to-top function.
* Easy access to practical party details.
* Ability to revisit the invitation.
* Ability to skip or replay the opening animation.
* Clear indications of clickable elements.
* Meaningful loading, empty and error states.
* A consistent navigation system across pages.
* Correct browser back-button behaviour.

Avoid scroll hijacking, compulsory lengthy animations or unnecessary transitions that slow down returning visitors.

27. Website privacy

The website contains a private residential address, guest names and eventually personal photographs and confidential murder mystery information.

It should therefore be treated as a private invitation-only website, rather than a public promotional website.

Where technically feasible, implement a shared invitation access code or a securely validated private invitation link.

The invitation link should remain convenient to open and share with the intended guests, while discouraging access by unrelated visitors.

Where a shared site access mechanism is implemented, preserve the visual experience by incorporating it into the themed entrance or invitation reveal.

Requirements:

* No search engine indexing.
* Appropriate noindex directives.
* Avoid publishing private content in public previews or social-sharing metadata.
* Securely restrict access to private documents and photographs.
* Use HTTPS.
* Minimise personal data collection.
* Do not expose private guest information unnecessarily.
* Avoid third-party tracking or advertising scripts.

Understand that an unlisted URL and noindex directives alone do not provide genuine access protection.

If proper website-wide access protection is not feasible for the chosen free setup, explain the limitations clearly and propose an appropriate alternative before launch.

28. Accessibility

The website should remain usable by all guests.

Requirements include:

* Readable colour contrast.
* Adequate body text size.
* Keyboard-accessible navigation.
* Accessible labels on buttons and forms.
* Alternative text for meaningful images.
* Reduced-motion support.
* Visible focus states.
* Clear form validation.
* Accessible image viewing.
* Music disabled by default.
* No essential information communicated solely through colour.
* Important event details available as selectable text.
* Proper page headings and semantic website structure.

Decorative flourishes must not compromise usability.

29. Performance

The website should load smoothly on mobile data and ordinary home Wi-Fi.

Use:

* Optimised image formats.
* Responsive image sizing.
* Lazy loading of lower-page imagery.
* Efficient CSS and JavaScript.
* Sensible animation techniques.
* Limited unnecessary third-party dependencies.
* Optimised font loading.
* Appropriate caching.

Do not unnecessarily reduce the quality of invitation artwork to the point where its text becomes difficult to read.

Avoid large video backgrounds or unnecessarily heavy animated assets.
